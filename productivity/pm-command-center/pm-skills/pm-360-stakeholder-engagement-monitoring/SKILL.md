# Stakeholder Engagement Monitoring (pm-360)

## What It Does

Monitors stakeholder engagement and sentiment — tracks communications cadence, gathers structured feedback, identifies early signs of disengagement, and adjusts engagement strategies before relationships degrade.

## When to Use

- Projects with more than 5 stakeholders or 3 stakeholder groups
- When a key stakeholder has been unresponsive for more than one communication cycle
- After major project deliverables or phase transitions
- During conflict or change management situations where stakeholder support is at risk

## Inputs

- Stakeholder register with contact info, role, influence level, and engagement preference
- Communication plan (from pm-140)
- Recent project status reports and meeting outcomes
- Feedback received from stakeholders (formal or informal)

## Workflow

1. **Review the stakeholder register** — Pull the current stakeholder list (pm-140). Confirm contact info, role, influence/interest level, and preferred communication channel for each stakeholder. Identify stakeholders flagged as `at-risk` or `disengaged`.
2. **Check communication cadence** — For each stakeholder, compare actual communication frequency against the plan. Flag stakeholders who have not received a status update or direct communication within 1.5× the planned interval. Flag stakeholders who have declined or rescheduled more than two consecutive meetings.
3. **Analyze engagement signals** — Review recent interactions for sentiment cues: tone of email replies (brief vs. engaged), meeting participation (cameras on, questions asked), approval turnaround time, and responsiveness to requests. Rate each stakeholder's current engagement level (high / moderate / low / disengaged).
4. **Gather structured feedback** — Send a brief pulse survey or schedule a short check-in call with at-risk or disengaged stakeholders. Ask: (1) Are you getting the information you need? (2) Are there blockers or concerns? (3) How would you rate project health? (1–5). Log responses and compare to previous scores.
5. **Identify disengagement patterns** — Look for clusters: an entire stakeholder group (e.g., finance) pulling back, a single influential stakeholder going quiet, or a sudden shift in sentiment after a specific event. Document the pattern and its potential impact on the project.
6. **Adjust engagement strategy** — For disengaged stakeholders: escalate to the project sponsor for direct outreach, propose a different communication format (in-person vs. written), or renegotiate communication expectations. For moderately engaged stakeholders: increase touchpoints or switch to their preferred channel.
7. **Document and report** — Update the stakeholder register with current engagement scores, last contact date, and notes. Report engagement health trends to the project sponsor in the monthly status report. Escalate any stakeholder-related risks to the risk register.

## Outputs

- Updated stakeholder register with engagement scores and last-contact dates
- Engagement health trend report (monthly)
- Escalation notifications to sponsor for disengaged key stakeholders
- Adjusted communication plan entries for at-risk stakeholders
- Risk register entries for stakeholder-related threats to project success

## Verification

- Pull the stakeholder register — confirm every stakeholder has a last-contact date within the planned communication interval.
- Send a test pulse survey to an at-risk stakeholder; verify the response is logged and scored.
- Verify that stakeholders flagged as `disengaged` have an active re-engagement action in the communication plan.
- Confirm sponsor was notified when a key stakeholder's engagement score dropped from high to low.

## Tips

- Do not mistake busy stakeholders for disengaged ones — a senior executive who is consistently brief but supportive is different from one who is quietly withdrawing. Look for pattern changes, not absolute scores.
- Feedback scores below 3/5 should trigger a direct conversation, not just an email. A low score in writing often understates the concern.
- When multiple stakeholders from the same group are disengaging simultaneously, the issue is usually with the project direction, not with individual relationships. Address the root cause with the group lead.
- Document every re-engagement attempt — if you later need to escalate to the sponsor or executive, a clear log of outreach efforts strengthens your case.
