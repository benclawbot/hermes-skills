# Stakeholder Analysis (pm-020)

## What It Does

Identifies all project stakeholders and maps them on a power/interest grid to prioritize engagement, communication, and influence management throughout the project lifecycle.

## When to Use

- Immediately after project charter is drafted or approved
- When stakeholder landscape is unclear or complex
- Before developing the communication management plan
- When a project is experiencing resistance or buy-in issues

## Inputs

- Project charter and scope document
- Initial stakeholder list (from sponsor, team, or discovery sessions)
- Organizational chart or division/department map
- Any known resistance signals or historical project issues

## Workflow

1. **Brainstorm all stakeholders** — List every person, group, or organization impacted by or interested in the project. Include sponsors, end-users, regulators, suppliers, and indirect beneficiaries.
2. **Classify on power/interest grid** — Plot each stakeholder on a 2×2 grid: X-axis = Power (Low/High), Y-axis = Interest (Low/High). Quadrants: (1) Keep Satisfied (High Power/Low Interest), (2) Manage Closely (High Power/High Interest), (3) Monitor (Low Power/Low Interest), (4) Keep Informed (Low Power/High Interest).
3. **Validate completeness** — Review with sponsor and core team. Add missing stakeholders and challenge边缘 entries.
4. **Define engagement strategy per quadrant** — For Manage Closely: weekly or ad-hoc executive updates. For Keep Satisfied: inform only on major decisions. For Keep Informed: regular project newsletters. For Monitor: optional project webpage or dashboard.
5. **Assign ownership** — Map each stakeholder or group to a responsible relationship owner (PM or sponsor). Document escalation paths.
6. **Document in stakeholder register** — Capture: name, role, organization, power level, interest level, engagement strategy, communication frequency, and owner.
7. **Review quarterly** — Stakeholder landscape evolves; re-map after major milestones or organizational changes.

## Output

- Stakeholder register (spreadsheet or markdown table)
- Power/interest grid visualization (2×2 diagram)
- Engagement strategy summary per stakeholder group
- Communication responsibility matrix ( RACI-adjacent)

## Tips

- Over-inclusivity in brainstorming is better — it's easier to drop stakeholders than discover you missed a powerful one late.
- The sponsor is almost always in the "Manage Closely" quadrant — treat them accordingly.
- Regulators and legal counsel often fall in "Keep Satisfied" but can become "Manage Closely" quickly if issues arise.
- If someone is both high-power and resistant, you need the sponsor to lead the relationship, not you alone.
- Include the PMO or governance body in the register — they are stakeholders even if they seem neutral.
