# Benefits Realization Tracking (pm-370)

## What It Does

Tracks realized benefits against the planned business case — monitors KPIs, confirms benefit ownership, reports actual vs. expected outcomes, and flags gaps for corrective action before the benefits window closes.

## When to Use

- After a project delivers its outputs and the operational phase begins
- At defined benefit measurement checkpoints (30, 60, 90, 180 days post-launch)
- When the business case expected specific revenue, cost, or efficiency gains
- During project closure and transition to BAU (business as usual)

## Inputs

- Approved business case with planned benefits, KPIs, and target dates
- Benefits register mapping each benefit to an owner, target value, and measurement method
- Post-launch operational data (sales, cost, usage, satisfaction metrics)
- Transition plan from project to BAU operations

## Workflow

1. **Confirm benefit ownership at project close** — Before the project team disbands, ensure every benefit in the register has a named business owner (not the project manager). If a benefit has no owner, escalate to the sponsor — it will not be tracked otherwise.
2. **Set baseline measurements** — Capture the pre-project baseline for each KPI (e.g., current call handle time, monthly churn rate, process cycle time). Without a baseline, realized benefit cannot be measured. Document baselines in the benefits register.
3. **Define measurement checkpoints** — Align with benefit owners to define 30/60/90/180-day measurement windows. Each checkpoint should have: metrics to measure, data source, measurement method, and who is responsible for extracting and reporting the data.
4. **Collect data at each checkpoint** — At each checkpoint, pull the relevant operational data, calculate the actual KPI value, and compare to the baseline and the target from the business case. Compute the gap: realized minus planned.
5. **Assess benefit realization status** — Categorize each benefit: On Track (realized ≥ 90% of target), At Risk (60–89% of target), Behind (realized < 60% of target), or Not Realized (benefit will not be achieved). Document rationale and supporting data.
6. **Investigate and remediate gaps** — For At Risk or Behind benefits, work with the benefit owner to identify root causes. Common causes: benefit was poorly defined, adoption was lower than expected, external market changes, or implementation gaps. Define a remediation plan with an owner and a revised target date.
7. **Report to stakeholders** — Produce a Benefits Realization Report at each checkpoint. Show: benefit name, owner, baseline, target, actual, gap, status, and remediation actions. Distribute to the project sponsor and steering committee.
8. **Close or carry forward benefits** — When a benefit is fully realized, close it in the register with final values and sign-off from the benefit owner. If a benefit requires ongoing tracking beyond the project lifecycle, transfer ownership to BAU and set a recurring reporting cadence.

## Outputs

- Benefits Realization Report (checkpoint cadence)
- Updated benefits register with actual vs. planned values
- Remediation plans for at-risk or behind-target benefits
- Benefit ownership transfer documentation to BAU
- Final benefit closure sign-off

## Verification

- Pull the benefits register after project close — confirm every benefit has a named business owner and a measurement checkpoint within 30 days.
- Verify a baseline exists for every KPI — without a baseline, benefit realization cannot be calculated.
- Test the checkpoint report: confirm actual values are sourced from operational systems, not estimated by the project team.
- Verify that benefits categorized as Behind have an active remediation plan and owner assigned.

## Tips

- Benefits tracking fails when it is treated as a project activity. Engage business owners and tie tracking to their operational cadence — they own the numbers, not the PMO.
- If a benefit cannot be measured, it should not be in the business case. Use this as a forcing function to tighten benefit definitions at project initiation (pm-040).
- Some benefits take time to materialize after go-live (learning curve, adoption curve). Use a trailing average or a ramp period rather than measuring the first week in isolation.
- When the business case anticipated a benefit but it is not being realized, do not let the project team argue it is "still coming." Challenge the benefit owner to provide evidence and a revised date — or formally close the benefit as not realized.
