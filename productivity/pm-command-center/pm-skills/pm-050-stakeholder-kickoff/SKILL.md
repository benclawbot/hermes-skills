# Stakeholder Kickoff (pm-050)

## What It Does

Plans and facilitates an effective project kickoff meeting that aligns all stakeholders on the project vision, scope, roles, and ways of working — building shared understanding and early commitment before work begins.

## When to Use

- Immediately after the project charter is signed and the core team is assembled
- When bringing new stakeholders or team members onto an ongoing project
- When there is a new sponsor or significant organizational change affecting the project
- As a reset mechanism after a project phase gate or major pivot

## Inputs

- Approved project charter
- Stakeholder register and power/interest grid
- High-level project plan or milestone timeline
- Team norms or operating agreements template
- List of decision-making and escalation protocols
- Meeting logistics (invite, room/video link, agenda)

## Workflow

1. **Set meeting objectives** — Define what "success" looks like for the kickoff itself: stakeholder alignment, role clarity, committed timeline, and shared working norms. Keep to 3–4 objectives.
2. **Design the agenda** — Structure the meeting in three acts: (a) Why and What (charter review, business case, scope), (b) Who and How (roles, governance, team norms), (c) What's Next (immediate actions, first sprint/phase, checkpoint dates). Allocate 60% of time to (a) and (b), 40% to action planning.
3. **Prepare materials** — Slide deck or handout covering: project purpose, scope boundaries, key milestones, org chart with roles, decision rights, and a RACI summary. Distribute the charter in advance — do not read it aloud verbatim.
4. **Facilitate the meeting** — Run introductions (if new group). Present business context and charter highlights. Walk through scope and out-of-scope explicitly. Review governance and escalation. Open floor for questions. Close with a roundtable commitment exercise ("one word or one sentence: what will you contribute?").
5. **Document decisions and agreements** — Capture decisions made, open issues, and action items. Distribute meeting notes within 24 hours.
6. **Establish team working agreements** — On the same day or in a follow-up session, agree on: communication channels and cadence, meeting norms (punctuality, agenda required), document repository location, Definition of Done for deliverables, and escalation protocol.
7. **Follow up with commitment confirmations** — Send a brief email to each key stakeholder confirming their role and asking them to reply with their commitment. This creates a paper trail and personal accountability.
8. **Schedule the next checkpoint** — Set the date for the next steering committee or project status meeting before closing. Momentum dies when there is no next date on the calendar.

## Output

- Approved meeting agenda and slide deck
- Meeting notes with decisions, action items, and owners
- Signed or confirmed stakeholder commitment acknowledgments
- Team working agreements document
- Scheduled next checkpoint or steering committee date
- Project repository initialized with kickoff materials

## Tips

- Kickoffs are not status meetings — they are alignment and commitment events. No work updates unless the team is new to an ongoing effort.
- If the sponsor does not attend, the kickoff signals the wrong priority. Reschedule until they can attend or have a recorded message from them.
- Remote kickoffs require extra facilitation structure — use breakout rooms for role discussions and a shared parking-lot doc for questions.
- The PM should not lecture — facilitate. Draw out the stakeholders, especially the sponsor and end-user representatives.
- Capture the energy of the kickoff and use it later when motivation wanes. Reference "as we agreed at kickoff..." to reinforce commitments.
- If someone raises a red-line issue (major scope concern, unresolved dependency), park it immediately and follow up offline — do not let it derail the session.
