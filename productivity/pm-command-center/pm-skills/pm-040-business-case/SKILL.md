# Business Case (pm-040)

## What It Does

Develops a structured business case that quantifies the financial and strategic value of the project, establishes ROI expectations, and provides the justification narrative for funding approval and executive commitment.

## When to Use

- Before funding is committed or budget is allocated
- When a formal ROI or payback period is required by organizational governance
- As input to the project charter and feasibility study
- When the project is large enough to require CFO or board approval

## Inputs

- Feasibility study findings
- Preliminary cost estimates (capital and operational)
- Revenue or savings projections (from finance or business unit)
- Strategic plan or departmental objectives document
- Comparable project benchmarks or industry data
- Constraints document (budget ceiling, resource limits)

## Workflow

1. **Define the investment scope** — Clarify what is being funded: software, hardware, headcount, external services, and one-time vs. recurring costs. Get finance to validate cost categories.
2. **Quantify costs** — Build a cost model: capital expenditure (CAPEX), operational expenditure (OPEX), and indirect costs (overhead, opportunity cost). Show totals by year if multi-year.
3. **Quantify benefits** — Categorize as tangible (quantifiable in currency) and intangible (strategic, reputational, risk-reduction). Assign monetary values where possible; flag estimates clearly.
4. **Calculate financial metrics** — Compute: Net Present Value (NPV), Internal Rate of Return (IRR), Payback Period, and Cost-Benefit Ratio. Use a standard discount rate (usually organizational WACC or treasury rate).
5. **Conduct sensitivity analysis** — Model optimistic, base, and pessimistic scenarios. Identify which assumptions have the greatest impact on ROI. Show break-even point.
6. **Map to strategic objectives** — Connect the project to stated organizational priorities (digital transformation, customer experience, compliance, growth). Use the language of the strategic plan.
7. **Document risks and mitigations** — Highlight the top 3–5 risks to realizing the business case and what reduces them.
8. **Format for the audience** — Executive summaries should fit on one page. Financial detail can be in appendices. Lead with the recommendation.
9. **Get finance sign-off** — Have the CFO or finance representative validate the cost and benefit figures before presenting to governance.

## Output

- Business case document (executive summary + detailed analysis, 3–6 pages)
- Financial model or spreadsheet (NPV, IRR, payback period calculations)
- Scenario analysis summary (optimistic/base/pessimistic)
- Finance validation sign-off
- Presentation deck (if required for steering committee or board)

## Tips

- Tangible benefits must be measurable and time-bound — "improved customer experience" alone is not enough.
- If the business case shows negative NPV, the project needs a strong strategic rationale to proceed — document it explicitly.
- Opportunity cost is real — include the cost of NOT doing the project (status quo continuation cost).
- Finance teams will challenge assumptions — pre-validate them before the formal review.
- Sensitivity analysis is not optional for large investments — it shows you have done due diligence.
- Keep the executive summary to 5–7 bullet points — decision-makers rarely read beyond it.
