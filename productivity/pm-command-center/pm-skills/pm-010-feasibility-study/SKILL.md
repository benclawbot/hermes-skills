# Feasibility Study (pm-010)

## What It Does

Conducts a structured 5-dimension feasibility assessment to determine whether a project should proceed. Evaluates technical, economic, legal, operational, and schedule dimensions to identify showstoppers and risk areas before commitment.

## When to Use

- New project proposal requires go/no-go decision
- Stakeholders need evidence of viability before approving funding
- High-risk or large-scale initiative where failure would be costly
- Before a project charter is finalized and signed

## Inputs

- Project concept document or problem statement
- Preliminary scope and objectives
- Available resources and constraints
- Initial stakeholder list
- Assumptions register (if any)

## Workflow

1. **Define the assessment scope** — Confirm project boundaries, success criteria, and decision criteria with sponsor. Set the bar for "go" vs. "no-go" outcomes.
2. **Technical feasibility** — Evaluate technology readiness, architecture fit, integration complexity, and internal capability. Identify gaps and dependencies.
3. **Economic feasibility** — Quantify cost estimates (CAPEX/OPEX), revenue or savings potential, and cost-of-delay. Calculate preliminary ROI range.
4. **Legal/regulatory feasibility** — Assess licensing, compliance, data privacy, IP, and contractual constraints. Flag any regulatory approval timelines.
5. **Operational feasibility** — Review process fit, change management impact, training needs, and organizational readiness. Confirm there is a process owner and operational team willing to adopt.
6. **Schedule feasibility** — Estimate timeline feasibility given scope, resources, and external dependencies. Identify hard deadlines and critical path risks.
7. **Synthesize findings** — Score each dimension (e.g., High/Medium/Low risk) and document showstoppers. Produce a one-page feasibility summary with a recommendation.

## Output

- Feasibility study report (2–4 pages) covering all 5 dimensions
- Risk and assumption log highlighting open items
- Go/No-Go recommendation with rationale
- List of conditions or mitigations required for a "Go" decision

## Tips

- Use a simple RAG (Red/Amber/Green) score per dimension — avoid false precision.
- Be honest about "medium" flags — they become contract clauses or workarounds later.
- Engage a technical peer reviewer for the technical dimension; don't rely solely on sponsor judgment.
- Schedule feasibility should include a bottoms-up estimate, not just a top-down guess.
- If all 5 dimensions are amber or green, the project is ready for chartering.
