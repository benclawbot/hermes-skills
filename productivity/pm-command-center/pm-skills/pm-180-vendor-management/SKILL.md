# PM Skill: Vendor Management (pm-180)

Vendors are extensions of the project team. Poor vendor management creates cost overruns, delays, and quality failures. This skill covers the full vendor lifecycle from selection through contract closeout.

## When to Use This Skill

- Procuring goods or services from external suppliers
- Managing an existing vendor relationship
- Evaluating vendor performance
- Resolving vendor disputes or changes
- Renewing or terminating vendor contracts

## Workflow

### 1. Define Procurement Requirements

Before contacting vendors:
- Document scope of work (SOW) or technical requirements
- Define acceptance criteria and deliverables
- Establish timeline and milestones
- Set budget range or firm budget
- Identify risks and constraints
- Determine contract type preference (fixed price, time & materials, etc.)

### 2. Vendor Selection

**RFP/RFI Process**:
- Issue Request for Proposal (RFP) or Information (RFI)
- Minimum 3 vendors for competitive bidding
- Score proposals against weighted criteria:
  - Technical capability (30-40%)
  - Cost (25-35%)
  - Past performance / references (15-20%)
  - Timeline feasibility (10-15%)
  - Risk profile (5-10%)

**Selection Steps**:
1. Screening: Verify minimum qualifications
2. Technical evaluation: Score against criteria
3. Commercial evaluation: Total cost of ownership
4. Shortlist: Top 2-3 vendors for deeper evaluation
5. Oral presentation or site visit (for critical vendors)
6. Reference checks
7. Final selection and negotiation

### 3. Contract Negotiation

Contract must address:
- **Scope**: Detailed SOW with inclusions/exclusions
- **Deliverables**: Clear, measurable, time-bound
- **Payment terms**: Milestone-based preferred, not all-at-once
- **Acceptance criteria**: How deliverables are validated
- **IP ownership**: Who owns deliverables, data, work product
- **Warranties**: Duration and scope of warranty period
- **SLAs**: Service level agreements with remedies
- **Change control**: How scope changes are priced and approved
- **Termination**: Grounds, notice period, wind-down obligations
- **Liability**: Caps, indemnities, insurance requirements
- **Dispute resolution**: Escalation path before litigation

### 4. Vendor Relationship Management

**Onboarding**:
- Kick-off meeting with project team and vendor
- Confirm communication channels and cadences
- Share project plan, WBS, and integration points
- Establish escalation contacts and procedures

**Ongoing**:
- Weekly or bi-weekly status check-ins
- Track deliverable progress against milestones
- Monitor quality against acceptance criteria
- Manage change orders promptly
- Maintain relationship health: address friction early

**Performance Monitoring**:
| Metric | Target | Frequency |
|--------|--------|-----------|
| On-time delivery | > 90% | Per milestone |
| Quality / rework rate | < 5% | Per deliverable |
| Budget variance | < 5% | Monthly |
| Communication responsiveness | < 24h | Per incident |

### 5. Vendor Issues and Dispute Resolution

**Early Warning Signs**:
- Missed milestones without warning
- Key personnel changes on vendor side
- Quality degradation
- Unusual budget claims or change requests
- Communication breakdown

**Resolution Steps**:
1. Document issue with evidence
2. Raise with vendor account manager
3. Escalate to vendor senior management if unresolved
4. Invoke contract dispute clause if necessary
5. Consider contract termination with cause if systemic

### 6. Contract Closeout

Upon completion:
- Verify all deliverables accepted
- Final payment processed (retainage if applicable)
- Performance review documented
- Lessons learned captured
- Contract filed in vendor archive
- Relationship maintained for future engagements

## Common Pitfalls

- **No clear SOW**: Scope ambiguity leads to disputes
- **Lowest cost selection**: Cheapest vendor often most expensive in the long run
- **Verbal commitments**: Everything must be in writing
- **Hands-off management**: Vendor relationships require active oversight
- **Late change orders**: Change control done after the fact is costly
- **Ignoring warning signs**: Vendor issues rarely resolve themselves

## Verification Steps

1. All vendors have signed contracts before work begins
2. SOWs have measurable acceptance criteria
3. Weekly vendor status meetings scheduled and attended
4. Performance metrics tracked per milestone
5. Change orders documented and approved before additional work
6. Contract closeout checklist completed
