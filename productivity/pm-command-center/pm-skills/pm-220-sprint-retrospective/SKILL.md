# PM Skill: Sprint Retrospective (pm-220)

The retrospective is the team's opportunity to inspect and adapt their process. It is the most important ceremony for continuous improvement. Without reflection, the same mistakes repeat sprint after sprint. A good retrospective produces concrete, actionable improvements — not a list of complaints.

## When to Use This Skill

- At the end of each sprint, after the sprint review
- When team velocity or morale is declining
- After a particularly difficult sprint (critical release, major incident)
- When the team has grown or changed (new members, new tools)
- Before implementing significant process changes

## Workflow

### 1. Pre-Retrospective Preparation

**Logistics**:
- Schedule 1.5-2 hours for a 2-week sprint (do not rush)
- Book a private space (no observers — team must be candid)
- Prepare retrospective board (physical or digital)
- Gather data: sprint metrics, issue log, impediment summary

**The Scrum Master Facilitates** (not the PM, though they may overlap):
- Facilitator should not have a vested interest in specific outcomes
- If SM is too close to the issues, consider rotating facilitator
- Prepare the board categories in advance

**What to Prepare**:
- Sprint goal and whether it was achieved
- Velocity vs. previous sprints
- Impediments raised and resolved
- Any scope changes or surprises
- Team availability and any workload concerns

### 2. The Retrospective Meeting

**Duration**: 1.5-2 hours

**Five-Phase Structure**:

#### Phase 1: Set the Stage (10 min)

- Welcome and thank the team for participating
- Remind everyone of the confidentiality norm
- State the retrospective goal: improve process, not blame individuals
- Review sprint facts: goal, velocity, highlights, lowlights
- Ask: "What is the one thing that, if improved, would have made this sprint better?"

**Prime Directive** (read aloud):
> "Regardless of what we discover, we understand and truly believe that everyone did the best job they could, given what they knew at the time, their skills and abilities, the resources available, and the situation at hand."

#### Phase 2: Gather Data (20-30 min)

**Sprint Timeline**:
- Build a timeline of the sprint's key events
- Wins, losses, surprises, decisions, blockers
- What happened and when (not why yet)

**Metrics Review**:
- Velocity: planned vs. actual
- Story completion rate
- Impediment count and resolution time
- Blocked hours
- Any trend changes

#### Phase 3: Generate Insights (30-40 min)

**Ask "Why?" Repeatedly**:
- Identify root causes, not symptoms
- 5 Whys technique for major issues
- Distinguish between team-controlled and external factors

**Categorize Findings**:
- **Process**: How we work (ceremonies, planning, estimation)
- **Communication**: Information flow, documentation, meetings
- **Technical**: Tools, architecture, environment, code quality
- **Team Dynamics**: Collaboration, trust, workload balance
- **External**: Stakeholders, vendors, dependencies outside team's control

#### Phase 4: Decide Improvements (20-30 min)

**Select Top 1-3 Improvements**:
- Force priority — do not create a 15-item improvement list
- Choose improvements that will have measurable impact
- Focus on team-controlled factors first

**SMART Improvement Commitment**:
- Specific: What exactly will change?
- Measurable: How will we know it's improved?
- Assignable: Who owns this improvement?
- Realistic: Can we actually do this next sprint?
- Time-bound: By when will we see results?

**Improvement Examples**:
- Bad: "Improve code reviews" (vague)
- Good: "PR reviews must be completed within 4 hours of submission" (SMART)
- Bad: "Better planning" (vague)
- Good: "All stories must be refined to acceptance criteria before sprint planning starts" (SMART)

#### Phase 5: Close and Commit (10 min)

**Each Improvement Gets**:
- Improvement description
- Owner (one person accountable)
- Commitment: what will be different next sprint
- Success metric: how we'll measure improvement

**Team Commitment**:
- Team collectively commits to trying the improvements
- Improvements should be visible (posted on board, referenced in standups)
- Celebrate improvements from last retrospective that worked

### 3. Popular Retrospective Formats

**Start-Stop-Continue** (simple, beginner-friendly):
- What should we **start** doing?
- What should we **stop** doing?
- What should we **keep doing**?

**Mad-Sad-Glad** (emotions-based):
- What made us **mad**?
- What made us **sad**?
- What made us **glad**?

**4Ls** (rapid collection):
- **Liked**: What did we like about this sprint?
- **Learned**: What did we learn?
- **Lacked**: What was missing?
- **Longed for**: What did we wish we had?

**Sailboat** (visual, good for complex sprints):
- What propelled us forward? (winds)
- What held us back? (anchors)
- What risks do we see? (rocks)
- Where are we headed? (island goal)

### 4. Follow-Through

**Post-Retrospective**:
- Document the retrospective (what was discussed, improvements committed)
- Publish improvement list to team
- Add improvement items to sprint planning agenda
- At next retrospective: review improvement progress first

**Improvement Tracking**:
- If an improvement didn't work: analyze why, try a different approach
- If an improvement worked: consider making it permanent process
- Do NOT abandon improvements after one failed attempt (unless clearly wrong)

### 5. Facilitator Tips

- Stay neutral — facilitate, don't direct outcomes
- Keep energy up — retrospectives can get negative
- Watch for dominant voices — give space to quiet contributors
- Use silent brainstorming (write first, then discuss) to give everyone equal voice
- If conflict arises: acknowledge it, don't suppress it
- End on a positive note — the team should feel empowered, not beaten down

## Common Pitfalls

- **No follow-through**: Improvements identified but never tracked
- **Too many improvements**: Team overwhelmed, nothing changes
- **Blaming individuals**: Retrospective is about process, not people
- **No psychological safety**: Team afraid to be honest
- **Skip the retrospective**: "We don't have time" is a false economy
- **Same retro every sprint**: Vary the format to keep it fresh

## Verification Steps

1. Retrospective held within 2 days of sprint end
2. All team members participated
3. Data was gathered before jumping to solutions
4. Top 1-3 improvements selected with SMART criteria
5. Improvement owners assigned and commitments documented
6. Improvements reviewed at start of next retrospective
7. Process changes from previous retrospective were evaluated

## Tools

- Retrospective board (Retrium, MetroRetro, Jira retro plugin, physical whiteboard)
- Timer (for keeping phases on track)
- Sprint metrics dashboard
- Impediment and issue log summary
- Previous retrospective action items (for follow-through)

## Exit Criteria

- Sprint retrospective completed with full team participation
- Top 1-3 process improvements identified with SMART commitments
- Improvement owners assigned
- Retrospective documented and shared with team
- Improvements reviewed at next retrospective
